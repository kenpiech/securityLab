Why we built this

Big companies buy login protection from fraud vendors with six-figure contracts, implementation teams, and quarterly business reviews. Everyone else gets nothing, and attackers know it. Small apps are where stolen passwords get tested first.

We think the same protection should be a tool you can just pick up. Sign up, get a key, add a few lines after your login check. If it stops being useful, stop using it. No one will call you.

whereami is our first tool. It looks at where a login comes from (the network, the location, whether it’s a proxy or a hosting provider) and at what’s normal for that account, and gives you a clear answer: let them in, ask for a second factor, or block.

Fair questions

Do I need to switch login providers?

No. It works next to whatever you use now: Clerk, Auth0, Supabase, Firebase, or your own login code.

Will it lock out my real users?

Rarely. It’s tuned to ask for a second factor rather than block when it isn’t sure, and you choose what to do with each answer.

What if I go over my budget?

You set a monthly cap. When you hit it, checks pause and logins carry on as normal. No surprise bills.

Can my AI coding assistant set this up?

Yes. Everything, from signing up to getting a key, works through our API, and our docs are written to be read by agents.
