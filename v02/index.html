Laboratory Uncovers Flaw in Gateways Guarding the Nation’s Power Plants

Vendors Notified in Confidence; Remedy Issued Before Disclosure

By the securityLab Research Staff

Researchers at securityLab this week disclosed a grave weakness in an industrial gateway device found in power stations, water works, and factories across the continent. The flaw, had it been discovered first by ill-intentioned parties, would have permitted entry without a password.

The laboratory reported the matter privately to the manufacturer some ninety days prior, in keeping with its long-standing practice, and withheld public particulars until a remedy was in the hands of operators.

“We look for these things so that others need not learn of them the hard way,” said a member of the research staff, who described weeks of patient study of the device’s inner workings.

The finding is the fourteenth such advisory issued by the laboratory this year. Its staff divide their days between such investigations and the building of instruments that let defenders detect and repel intrusions of their own.

Operators are urged to apply the manufacturer’s update without delay. Full technical particulars appear in the laboratory’s published advisory.
