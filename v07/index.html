How it works

      
Check the login before you issue the session.

      
Your auth flow stays the same. Add one call after the password or magic link succeeds, and act on the answer.

      

        

          
01
Send the attempt

          
Pass the IP, user agent, and a stable account ID. Nothing else is required.

          
sl.whereami({ ip, userAgent, accountId })

        

        

          
02
Get a verdict

          
A risk score from 0 to 1, a recommended action, and the signals behind it.

          
allowchallengedeny

        

        

          
03
Act on it

          
Let good users straight in. Step up to MFA or email verification when risk is high.

          
if (r.action !== "allow") requireMfa()

        

      

    

  


  

    

      
Signals

      
What whereami looks at.

      

        
residential_proxy
Traffic relayed through hijacked home connections.

        
vpn · tor · hosting
Anonymizers and data-center ranges.

        
impossible_travel
Locations no human could move between in time.

        
new_network
First time this account has signed in from here.

        
ip_velocity
Many accounts tried from one address.

        
abuse_recent
Seen in credential stuffing in the last 24 hours.

        
ua_mismatch
Client claims that don’t match the network.

        
geo_precision
City-level location with a confidence radius.

FAQ
Questions developers ask.

      

        
Does this replace my auth provider?
No. whereami adds a risk signal to the authentication you already have. Keep Clerk, Auth0, Supabase, or your own login.

        
How fast is it?
Median response is under 20 ms from most regions, fast enough to run inline before issuing a session.

        
What data do you store?
We store the IP and a hashed account ID for 30 days to power account-level signals like new network detection. No passwords, ever.

        
Can an AI agent sign up?
Yes. Accounts, keys, and spend caps can all be created through the API. See llms.txt for a machine-readable summary.
