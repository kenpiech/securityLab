securityLab: 25 landing pages

Twenty-five versions of a landing page for a cybersecurity research and products company, built to explore widely and then narrow down. “securityLab” is a placeholder name; it is a design prototype, and every product, API, package, and price shown is a placeholder that does not exist.

Round 1 · Going wide

Five deliberately different directions in mood, era, and layout.

Defense Consolev01

Dark operations dashboard: terminal boot sequence, classification banners, sidebar index, red-white-blue accents.
The Heraldv02

Old-time broadsheet front page: blackletter masthead, research as news stories, products as period advertisements.
Nordic Studiov03

Scandinavian minimalism: warm off-white, muted palette, geometric print illustration, numbered single-column sections.
Ink & Brassv04

Modern and elegant: split screen with a fixed deep-green panel, refined serif type, ivory content column.
Fire Lookoutv05

Nature metaphor: a fire lookout tower watching the forest, park-service signage, elevation markers, a threat “danger dial.”

Round 2 · Real companies, different visitors

Round 1 looked like themed exercises, not companies: themes worn as costumes, no clear offer, no product, no trust signals. Round 2 starts from the actual product (whereami, login-time risk scoring, pay-as-you-go) and the bar “would this pass as a real production site?” Each version targets a different visitor.

Missionv06

Defense-tech tone (Palantir, Anduril): black, wide uppercase type, a quiet field of logins with a few turning red. Minimal text.
Developer-firstv07

Stripe/Resend structure: code in the hero with language tabs, integrations strip, how-it-works, signals grid, pricing calculator.
Agent-nativev08

For AI agents and vibe coders: a prompt to paste into your coding agent, a spec sheet, and a Human/Agent toggle that shows an llms.txt view.
Evidencev09

For technical evaluators: precision/recall chart, a live-looking stream of decisions, benchmark table, evaluation methodology.
Founder-ledv10

For small business owners: plain language, a letter on why it exists, enterprise vs. us comparison, pricing with real-world examples.

Round 3 · Narrowing: one style, five structures

v07’s visual style and v08’s agent-first content were the favorites, so round 3 combines them and holds the look constant. The positioning also got sharper: whereami detects residential proxy IPs and nothing more, which makes it a modular building block rather than an auth provider or a risk-score black box. The variable now is page structure.

Straight mergev11

v07’s layout carrying v08’s content: agent prompt, zero-to-key, spec sheet, and the Human/Agent toggle with an llms.txt view.
Docs as landing pagev12

Stripe-docs layout: sticky section sidebar, quickstart-style center column, and a code panel that follows the section you’re reading.
Narrativev13

One column read like a launch post: the correct-password attacker, a proxy diagram, one question and one field, your rules, small tools.
Playground firstv14

The hero is a working demo: pick or type an IP, see the response, a plain-English answer, and the matching cURL. Explanation comes after.
Audience splitv15

Leads with the company. A Developers / AI agents / Companies switch swaps the main panel, followed by a catalog of the tools.

Round 4 · Converging on v12

v12’s docs-as-landing-page structure won: it feels like the product is already in your hands, and the dark code panel shows the product instead of describing it. From here, each version makes one deliberate change.

Wordmarkv16

v12 with the “securityLab” wordmark carried over from v09: “Lab” set in the accent red.
